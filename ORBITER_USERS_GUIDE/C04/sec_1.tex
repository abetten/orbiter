Orbiter commands to create a finite projective space $\PG(n,q)$ 
are listed in Table~\ref{tab:PG:create}.
The command also creates the group of the space, 
which is $\PGGL(n+1,q).$
\orbitertableofcommands{projective_space_with_action}
Let us look at a very simple example. 
Suppose we want to create $\PG(3,2).$ 
The following command sequence creates the finite field $\bbF_2$ 
and stores the object in the symbol table, using the label $F$.
After that, the projective space $\PG(3,F)$ is created, using the field $F$. 
The projective space is 
stored in the symbol table under the label $P$.

\sourcecode{PG_3_2_easy}

Orbiter offers 
indexing for points and for subspaces o allf $\PG(n,q)$ of a fixed dimension. 
The incidence matrix between points and lines with respect to this ordering can be 
computed. 
The indexing is used to establish 
the permutation representations of the projective group, 
as will be described in Section~\ref{sec:lineargroups}.
The indexing of points is not the lexicographic ordering. 
It emphasizes the role of frames in the geometry
by assigning the smallest rank values to the members of the standard frame. 
After that, the other points are listed. 

\bigskip


Orbiter can create a cheat sheet for $\PG(n,q).$ 
The cheat sheet lists the objects in the space, and basic properties.
Here are two examples. 
The first is a projective plane. The second is a projective 3-space. 

\bigskip


The following command creates a cheat sheet 
for $\PG(2,4)$:

\sourcecode{PG_2_4}

The cheat sheet contains a drawing of the plane:
\orbiteroutput{GRAPHICS/WRAPPERS/PG_2_4}
The affine plane is shown in the cartesian plane, 
while the line at infinity 
is wrapped around the top right corner.
The cheat sheet continues by listing the points, including the 
canonical Baer subgeometry $\PG(2,2).$
After that, the points are listed again, 
but with left-normalized vectors.
Finally, the lines are shown. 
\orbiteroutput{GRAPHICS/LATEX/report_PG_2_4}



\bigskip

The following command creates a cheat sheet for $\PG(3,2).$

\sourcecode{PG_3_2}

The cheat sheet shows points, lines and planes. 
The lines are shown together with their Pl\"ucker coordinates. 
The lines whose Pl\"ucker coordinates are unit vectors are shown separately.
\orbiteroutput{GRAPHICS/LATEX/report_PG_3_2}
